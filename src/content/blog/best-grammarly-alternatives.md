Grammarly works. That's not the problem. The problem is that it's another $12–$30 monthly subscription that watches everything you type, and for a lot of writing it's more than you actually need. If you're a bootstrapper counting every recurring charge, or you just want something private, free, or better suited to long-form work, you're looking for the best Grammarly alternatives — and there are a few genuinely good ones in 2026.

Here's the short version. This guide skips the padded "18 tools" listicle and focuses on what matters when you're choosing one to actually use every day — real cost, how usable the free tier is, and what each tool replaces. I compared these on public pricing and feature data as of July 2026, plus hands-on familiarity with the mainstream options. I didn't run a 12-month lab test of every tier, so confirm the current price on the official page before you pay.

## Why people replace Grammarly in 2026

Three reasons come up again and again, and they point to different alternatives.

The first is cost. Grammarly Premium is a recurring monthly bill, and if you only need clean spelling and grammar, you're overpaying. A free tier or a one-time license does that job without the subscription.

The second is privacy. Grammarly sends your text to its servers to analyze it. For sensitive drafts — client work, legal, unpublished manuscripts — a lot of writers would rather use a tool that processes locally or explicitly doesn't store their text.

The third is fit. Grammarly is a generalist. If you write novels, it barely understands pacing and repetition. If you write in five languages, its coverage is thin. If you just want punchy, readable prose, it won't teach you to cut. This is where most roundups get it wrong — they rank tools head-to-head when the honest answer is that the right pick depends on your use case.

## How I picked these alternatives

Every tool below had to clear a few bars that matter to a real writer, not a marketing page.

- **A usable free tier or fair pricing** — either genuinely free for the basics, or priced so it doesn't feel like a tax.
- **Actually good at its core job** — catches real errors, not just the obvious ones.
- **A clear strength** — it should beat Grammarly at something specific, whether that's price, privacy, languages, or depth.
- **No lock-in games** — transparent plans, and ideally a browser extension so it works where you write.

I weighted "what does this replace, and is it worth it" above raw feature counts, because the tool you'll actually keep using beats the one with the longest spec sheet.

## LanguageTool — best free and most private

If I had to hand one alternative to most people, it's LanguageTool. The free tier covers spelling, punctuation, and some style, it's open source with the code on GitHub, and the browser add-on states that it doesn't store your text — which is the privacy angle Grammarly can't match. It also supports 30-plus languages and dialects, including six English varieties, so it's the obvious pick if you write in more than one language.

The free plan checks a limited chunk of text at a time, and Premium (roughly $5 per month on an annual plan, per third-party roundups) lifts the limits and adds deeper style and rephrasing suggestions. For a lot of writers, though, the free tier alone is a complete Grammarly replacement. Confirm the current limits and price on the official [LanguageTool site](https://languagetool.org/).

## ProWritingAid — best for long-form and authors

ProWritingAid is the one I'd point novelists, non-fiction authors, and anyone writing 5,000-plus words toward. It comes closest to matching Grammarly on core corrections, then pulls ahead with 25-plus writing analysis reports — pacing, repeated words, sentence variety, dialogue tags — that Grammarly simply doesn't do.

The free plan is capped at 500 words per check, which is fine for testing but too tight for real work. Premium is $30 per month billed monthly, or $120 per year (about $10 a month) — and the reason I keep recommending it to subscription-averse writers is the lifetime license at $399 one-time for Premium (Premium Pro is more). Buy once, and you never see the bill again. For an author who edits a book a year, that math works out fast. Prices are on the official [ProWritingAid pricing page](https://prowritingaid.com/pricing).

## QuillBot — best free grammar plus paraphrasing

QuillBot quietly has one of the strongest free tiers here. Its grammar checker is genuinely free and unlimited with no sign-up, across six-plus languages, and its error detection now rivals Grammarly for everyday writing. On top of that it's a purpose-built paraphraser with multiple rewriting modes, which makes it a favorite for ESL writers and students who need to rework a sentence, not just fix it.

Premium runs around $8 per month on an annual plan (with a cheaper student rate) and unlocks longer paraphrasing, more modes, and the summarizer. If your two needs are "check my grammar for free" and "help me rephrase this," QuillBot covers both in one place. I dug into the free grammar tool specifically, and for casual use it's hard to argue with free and unlimited.

## Hemingway Editor — best for readable, concise prose

Hemingway is a different kind of tool, and that's the point. Instead of chasing every comma, it grades your readability and flags long sentences, passive voice, and needless adverbs so you learn to write tighter. The web app is free and does the core job with no account, which is why I recommend it as a free companion to any grammar checker rather than a full replacement.

Hemingway Editor Plus adds grammar fixes and AI tone adjustments for roughly $8–$12 per month depending on the tier, and there's a separate desktop version. If your writing problem is "too wordy" rather than "too many typos," Hemingway fixes the thing Grammarly mostly ignores. Check current tiers on the official [Hemingway site](https://hemingwayapp.com/).

## Microsoft Editor — best if you already pay for Microsoft 365

Here's the one most roundups skip because it isn't flashy. If you already use Microsoft 365, you already have Microsoft Editor. The basics — spelling, grammar, and a browser extension for Edge and Chrome — are free, and the advanced style suggestions come bundled with a 365 subscription you're probably already paying for. It's not the deepest checker here, but for anyone living inside Word, the correct move is often to turn on the tool you already own before buying a new one. If 365 is already in your budget, this is the one alternative that lets you skip a separate subscription entirely.

## Which Grammarly alternative should you pick?

If I had to make it simple, it comes down to your main reason for switching.

Want it free and private? Start with LanguageTool, and add Hemingway's free web app for readability. Write long-form or books? ProWritingAid, and buy the lifetime license if you write regularly. Need free grammar plus rewriting? QuillBot. Already paying for Microsoft 365? Turn on Microsoft Editor before you spend another cent. And if you want AI-style rewriting and tone control, tools like Wordtune and DeepL Write are worth a look, though they solve rephrasing more than proofreading.

For most people, picking one free tier or one modest plan is the right call. You do not need to stack three writing subscriptions. ## FAQ

**Q. What is the best free Grammarly alternative?**
A. LanguageTool and QuillBot are the two strongest free options. LanguageTool is open source, private, and multilingual, while QuillBot's grammar checker is free and unlimited with no sign-up. Hemingway's free web app is a great readability companion to either.

**Q. Is there a Grammarly alternative with no subscription?**
A. Yes. ProWritingAid sells a one-time lifetime license (around $399 for Premium as of July 2026), so you pay once instead of monthly. The free tiers of LanguageTool, QuillBot, and Hemingway's web app also cover the basics with no recurring charge.

**Q. What is the best Grammarly alternative for authors and long-form writing?**
A. ProWritingAid. Its 25-plus writing reports on pacing, repetition, and sentence variety are built for long documents, which is exactly where Grammarly is weakest. The lifetime license also suits writers who edit book-length work every year.

**Q. Can I fully replace Grammarly for free?**
A. For everyday spelling, grammar, and style, yes — LanguageTool's free tier plus Hemingway or QuillBot covers most writers. If you need deep long-form reports or heavy AI rewriting, a paid plan or lifetime license is worth it, but many people never need to pay.
