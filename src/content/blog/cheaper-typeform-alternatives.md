## Why does Typeform get expensive so fast?

The short answer: responses are metered, and the meter is low. Typeform's free plan is capped tight — recent pricing has listed as few as ~10 responses per month, and older tiers listed around 100, so sources genuinely disagree here; either way it's small. The entry paid tier then runs about $28/month billed annually for roughly 100 responses, shared across every form you own. Send one popular survey and you blow through that in a day. Because that exact number moves, confirm the current cap on the official page before you decide.

This is where most "cheaper Typeform alternatives" roundups get it wrong. They line tools up by monthly sticker price, but for a form builder the sticker price barely matters — a $10 plan that caps you at 100 responses can cost more in practice than a free plan with no cap. What decides your real bill is the response ceiling and what sits behind the paywall.

So I reordered this whole comparison around one question a form actually forces you to answer: how many submissions can I collect before this stops being free? You can confirm Typeform's current numbers yourself on the [official pricing page](https://www.typeform.com/pricing/) — but as of writing, that cap is exactly what the alternatives below are built to beat.

## Tally — the default cheaper switch

If you want the closest thing to a free Typeform, start with Tally. Its free plan includes unlimited forms and unlimited responses, and — unusually — it doesn't lock the useful stuff behind a paywall. Conditional logic, calculated fields, file uploads, and Stripe payment collection are all on the free tier.

The editor is the other reason it clicks for people leaving Typeform. It works like a document — closer to Notion than a drag-and-drop builder — so you just start typing to build a form. For most solo founders and small teams, that combination of "unlimited free" plus "no feature gating" makes it the obvious first stop.

The paid plan starts at around $29/month as of July 2026, mostly to remove Tally branding and add pro touches. But here's the honest part: a lot of people never need to pay, because the free tier already does what Typeform charges for. That's the whole "cheaper" story in one tool.

## Fillout and Zoho Forms — generous free, low paid entry

If Tally isn't your style, Fillout is the next stop. Its free plan gives 1,000 responses per month with unlimited forms, plus multi-page forms, conditional logic, and payment collection — genuinely capable, not a stripped teaser. Paid plans start around $15/month (as of July 2026), which is well under Typeform's entry tier for far more responses.

Zoho Forms is worth a look if you want the cheapest paid ladder rather than a free-forever plan. Its free tier is more modest (around 500 responses per month), but its paid entry sits near $10/month, one of the lowest in this category. If you're already in the Zoho ecosystem, that integration is a bonus.

## Formbricks — the open-source, self-hostable pick

If you care about owning your data, Formbricks is the one open-source option worth a solo operator's time. It's a privacy-first survey platform you can self-host for free, forever, which removes both the response cap and the per-seat meter entirely. For anyone with GDPR concerns or a preference for keeping form data on their own infrastructure, that's a real edge Typeform reserves for enterprise.

Even without self-hosting, its cloud free tier is more generous than Typeform's, and mid-tier plans give more responses per dollar. The honest caveat: self-hosting means you're responsible for updates and uptime, so weigh that maintenance against the money saved. I didn't run a long-term reliability test, so if uptime is critical, a hosted free tier like Tally or Fillout is the calmer route.

Between these, the choice is about priorities. Want the least friction? Tally. Want maximum free capacity with power features? Fillout. Want to own the data? Formbricks. All three undercut Typeform for the way most people actually use forms.

## What to skip when you're chasing "cheaper"

Skip tools whose free plan is really a trial in disguise. Some form builders advertise a free tier but cap it at 25–30 responses per survey, which is low enough that you'll be pushed to pay almost immediately — that's not cheaper, it's a countdown. Check the monthly response number, not just the word "free."

Also be cautious with per-seat pricing if you're solo. A few alternatives price by user, which is irrelevant when you're a team of one but quietly inflates the bill the moment you add a collaborator. For a single operator, a flat free tier beats a low per-seat rate almost every time — the same logic that drives choosing [Airtable alternatives for solo founders](/airtable-alternatives-solo-founders/) applies here.

And don't over-buy on features. Typeform's polish is real, but if you're leaving over price, you probably don't need enterprise logic jumps and advanced analytics. Match the tool to the job — collecting responses without a wall — and the cheaper option is usually the simpler one.

## How to choose — a quick decision path

If I had to pick one path for most people, it's this. Start with the most generous free tier and only pay when a real limit forces you.

Walk it in order: **① Just need to collect responses without a cap?** Use Tally — unlimited free. **② Want a generous free tier with advanced features?** Fillout, 1,000 free responses. **③ Need to own or self-host the data?** Formbricks, open source. **④ Want the cheapest paid ladder for later?** Zoho Forms near $10/month. **⑤ Only running a quick internal survey?** Google Forms, free and done.

Pricing changes often in this category, so treat every number here as "confirm before you pay." But the structure holds: the cheapest Typeform alternative for you is the one whose free response cap outlasts your actual usage.
